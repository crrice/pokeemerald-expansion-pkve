# Gameplay Roadmap: Act 1 (Gyms 1 & 2)

This document outlines the intended player progression, availability of key resources, and design rationale for the first act of the game, culminating in the defeat of the second Gym Leader, Brawly.

## Core Design Principles

This roadmap is guided by several core design philosophies:

1.  **Thematic Progression (Weather):** The player should be introduced to the hack's core mechanic—weather—in a structured way. The progression should follow a "Problem -> Tools -> Mastery" arc, where they first experience weather as an obstacle, then acquire the tools to counter it, and finally gain the ability to control it themselves.
2.  **Rival 'Fair Play' Doctrine:** The rival serves as a mirror to the player's journey. They should only use Pokémon that are rare but technically obtainable by the player in the same areas. This reinforces the narrative of two trainers on a parallel adventure. The rival getting "lucky" with a 1% encounter is a perfect way to introduce new mechanics.
3.  **Starter 'Early Power' Philosophy:** While the starter trio has distinct advantages and disadvantages against the Gyms, each starter should feel powerful and useful from the very beginning. They should be equipped with the tools to contribute meaningfully even in a bad match-up, rewarding the player's choice. The journey should feel like a series of trade-offs (struggle now, dominate later), not a punishment for a "wrong" choice.

## Segment 1: The Foundation
*(Littleroot Town → Rival Battle 1)*

**Player Journey:**
*   Choose a starter: Pikipek (Flying), Machop (Fighting), or Roggenrola (Rock).
*   Receive the Pokédex and travel to Route 103 for the first rival battle.

| Availability | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pokémon** | Starter only. |
| **Items** | None. |
| **Mechanics**| Basic battle mechanics. |

**Design Rationale:**
This segment isolates the player with their starter to build a foundational bond and understanding of their most important Pokémon. The rival battle is a simple, pure test of this initial experience.

## Segment 2: Building a Team
*(Post-Rival 1 → Wally's Tutorial)*

**Player Journey:**
*   Receive Poké Balls.
*   Travel through Routes 102 & 101 to Petalburg City.

| Availability | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pokémon** | Encounters on Routes 101-103 become catchable (Wooper, Wingull, Spinarak, etc.). |
| **Items** | Poké Balls, Potions. |
| **Mechanics**| Day/Night system becomes strategically relevant for encounters. |

**Design Rationale:**
The player is now tasked with building a team to solve their first major problem: the upcoming Rock-type Gym. Their starter choice dictates their immediate priorities.
*   **Pikipek players** are incentivized to explore and learn the Day/Night system to find and catch the deliberately-placed **Wooper** (Water/Ground), their key to victory against Roxanne.
*   **Machop and Roggenrola players** can find counters for future challenges (like Wingull or Spinarak).

## Segment 3: The First Lesson (Weather)
*(Post-Wally → Roxanne)*

**Player Journey:**
*   Complete Wally's tutorial in Petalburg.
*   Traverse Petalburg Woods and confront the first villainous team grunt.
*   Face the rival in a rematch on Route 116 before Rustboro City.

| Availability | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pokémon** | Petalburg Woods encounters (Shroomish, Cottonee, etc.). |
| **Items** | Exp. Share. |
| **Mechanics**| **First forced weather battle** via the rival. |

**Design Rationale:**
This segment introduces the core "problem" of the hack: weather. Adhering to the 'Fair Play' Doctrine:
*   The rival will have found a **"rare" weather-setting Pokémon** native to Hoenn.
    *   **Brendan (Aggro):** Has a **Torkoal** (1% encounter rate on Route 116) with the **Drought** ability, setting up harsh sun.
    *   **May (Stall):** Has a **Hippopotas** (1% encounter in the dusty area near Rusturf Tunnel) with **Sand Stream**, setting up a sandstorm.
*   The player is forced to overcome this new, difficult mechanic.
*   **Roxanne's Gym** now serves as a satisfying, straightforward "gear check" after the complex rival battle.
*   **Starter Buffs in Action:**
    *   **Pikipek:** Should learn **Rock Smash** via level-up before this point. This allows it to fight back against Roxanne's Rock-types, making it feel useful even in a disadvantageous match-up.
    *   **Machop:** Crushes the Rock-type gym, giving the player a feeling of immense power.
    *   **Roggenrola:** Has a neutral-to-positive match-up, representing the balanced path.

## Segment 4: Acquiring Tools & Counters
*(Post-Roxanne → Brawly)*

**Player Journey:**
*   Earn the Stone Badge and receive the Cut HM.
*   Travel to Dewford Town and explore Granite Cave.

| Availability | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pokémon** | **Oldale Ruins (Cut Area):** Rare **Psyduck** with **Cloud Nine** ability. <br> **Serene Pond (Cut Area):** **Lotad** (Rain Dish), **Poliwag** (Swift Swim). <br> **Granite Cave:** **Abra**, **Makuhita**. |
| **Items** | **Utility Umbrella** (Gift from Prof. Birch after beating the weather-using rival). |
| **Mechanics**| Rewarding exploration with direct counters to the game's core challenge. |

**Design Rationale:**
This segment is about empowering the player. After being shown the "problem" of weather, they are now given the "tools" to solve it.
*   **Rewarding Exploration:** The first direct weather counter (**Cloud Nine Psyduck**) and a key strategic item (**Utility Umbrella**) are deliberately placed as rewards for story progression and exploration (using Cut). This teaches the player to engage with the world to solve their problems.
*   **Foreshadowing:** The player can now find their own weather-synergy Pokémon (Lotad, Poliwag), foreshadowing that they will one day be able to control weather themselves.
*   **The Payoff/Trade-off:** Brawly's Fighting-type Gym is where the starter choice trade-offs come to fruition.
    *   **Pikipek players**, who struggled against Gym 1, now have a massive advantage, making their earlier hardship feel rewarding.
    *   **Machop players**, who breezed through Gym 1, now face a tougher challenge. They must rely on their team (like a caught Wingull) or a newly-acquired Abra. Ensuring Machop learns **Knock Off** early gives them a tool against Brawly's part-Psychic Meditite.
    *   **Roggenrola players** continue their balanced journey, facing another neutral challenge.
